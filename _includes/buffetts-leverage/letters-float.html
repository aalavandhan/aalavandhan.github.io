On float

Float is money we hold but don't own. In an insurance operation, float arises because premiums are received before losses are paid, an interval that sometimes extends over many years. During that time, the insurer invests the money.

Warren Buffett, 1998

This collect-now, pay-later model leaves us holding large sums – money we call “float” – that will eventually go to others. Meanwhile, we get to invest this float for Berkshire's benefit.

Warren Buffett, 2011

Float is better than free. In such years, we are actually paid for holding other people's money.

Warren Buffett, 2004
