<p class="kural"><span class="kural-n">1</span>Most stocks fall behind;<br>yet markets rise.</p>

Stock returns are skewed. [Most stocks underperform cash](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2900447); the best 4% create all the net wealth.

<p class="kural"><span class="kural-n">2</span>Few swans bring the wealth;<br>own the flock.</p>

Diversification isn't just about lowering risk. It also raises your typical return. The winners win big, and owning the whole market means you benefit from holding them.

---

In 1974 [Paul Samuelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Samuelson), the first American to win the Nobel Prize in economics, publicly called for someone to start an [index fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_fund).

Two years later [John Bogle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Bogle), the founder of Vanguard, launched one for ordinary investors. Wall Street called it [Bogle's Folly](https://www.ifa.com/articles/fifty_years_bogle_folly).

Samuelson later [ranked indexing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Bogle) alongside the invention of the wheel, the alphabet and Gutenberg's printing press. Fifty years on, it's how most people invest.
